# General particle-system trends

## Supported qualitative trends

The current evidence supports relative ordering statements, not universal absolute activation energies.

For fast firing, a crystalline particle system is more likely to show matched-density grain-size benefit when densifying-event nucleation has a finite waiting regime, a fast ramp crosses that regime more quickly than a slow ramp, and exchange/transport remain fast enough to finish densification events. Too-facile nucleation removes the history difference; excessively slow completion removes attainable matched-density support.

For two-step sintering, a system is more likely to show a finite window when the first step prepares a removable or shrinkable high-density pore inventory, low-T closed shrinkage/accommodation remains active, and boundary migration recovers more steeply with temperature. Too little shrinkage produces the lower failure; too much migration produces the upper failure.

The two behaviors do not require the same primary channel. In the present models, PR can be non-causal for fast firing while remaining necessary for two-step pore-store preparation.

## Common constraints

- Both compared paths must attain the scored density interval.
- State variables and stores must remain bounded and nonnegative.
- High-density scoring requires an explicit active support channel.
- A large grain-size separation is acceptable when it is attained and numerically supported.
- A finite process window requires both lower and upper boundaries.

## Limits to generalization

Candidate 693168's near-unity closed fraction and accommodation law are calibration-sensitive. The 73 joint successes are combinations within a promoted subset and do not establish universality. The fast and two-step layers are not dynamically coupled in this audit. Consequently, the trends are hypotheses for selecting experimental materials and measurements, not validation.

## Experimental discriminants

Use interrupted ramps to measure nucleation onset and pore/grain evolution; impedance, tracer, or relaxation experiments to constrain exchange and transport; 3D imaging or scattering to determine connected and closed pore fractions; and matched-T2 grain-growth and closed-pore shrinkage experiments. The most informative combined test measures the closed-pore inventory at the switch and then follows shrinkage and grain growth through several T2 values bracketing failure and success.
