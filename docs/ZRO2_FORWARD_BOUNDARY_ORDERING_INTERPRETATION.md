# Boundary-ordering interpretation

The lower density boundary lies 100–200 °C above the upper strict-growth boundary wherever both can be estimated. Thus low T2 preserves grain size only where density is insufficient, while T2 values capable of density attainment exceed the strict grain limit. A minimum 125 °C combined shift is required to produce a 25 °C sampled window.

The current evidence does not establish whether that motion should come from faster low-temperature open-pore removal, persistent pinning, lower grain mobility, closed-pore accommodation, or altered low-temperature activity. The OAT boundary maps are strongly censored and no case satisfies all physical-pathway gates. Treating a two-point response as a calibrated lever would be overfitting.

Barrier extrapolation, pore topology, and closed accommodation remain unresolved limitations. This is a negative thermal diagnostic, not validation.
