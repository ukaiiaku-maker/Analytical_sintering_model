# Fast-Firing Nucleation-Limited Parameter Map

The screen evaluated 2,304 reference/fast comparisons from 256 material sets. There were 1,089 fully attained comparisons, zero numerical-censored scores, 28 strict successes, and six distinct successful materials.

Successful examples concentrate in schedules with sufficient high-temperature exposure; the strongest repeated cases used a 500 kJ/mol nucleation barrier and the 1550 C/20 h schedule. Surface barriers varied, so this sample does not identify a unique surface activation energy. The result is controlled by the relative onset of the serial nucleation time and conservative PR/surface redistribution, not by a fast/slow flag.

The diagnostic interpretation is: slow ramps accumulate redistribution while densification activity is weak; fast ramps traverse that interval sooner. After nucleation activates, transport must be fast enough to attain the common density interval. PR moves pore volume between bins and never changes total pore volume directly.

Important caveat: the largest ratios are implausibly large and should be treated as stiff-model warnings, not preferred fits. Measurements needed next are matched-density mean-grain trajectories, pore D50/D90 and large-pore tails during interrupted ramps, a nucleation/activity proxy, and surface-area loss before densification onset.

The disabled-topology calculation is the fast-firing ablation. The present bounded screen did not separately rerun zero-PR and zero-nucleation-barrier campaigns; those remain required before causal validation.
